# Changing brakes on an Audi

Some days later I brought my car over to the Hyundai workshop for its yearly inspection and _of course_ it didn't pass (again). The reason this time: The brakes and brake Disks were completely worn down. _Wait... didn't Audi recently change my brake caliper? Didn't they see that the brakes were fully worn? **Why didn't they tell me?!**_

Nevermind, I was completely fed up and decided to give them brakes a complete overhaul on all wheels. New disks, new brake pads, everything. And I would do it on my own (or actually with another friend of mine) to save money. And there's where the actual painful journey begins...

## Those rear brake calipers...

To remove the rear brake disks, the Audi A4 B8 (specifically the B8!) requires the caliper brackets to be removed. This may arguably be the most idiotic design choice on the entire car. These parts were forged in the deepest of hell. _Trust me._

I asked my friend's dad and even his uncle to help me get that stupid bolt out - we tried with all of our combined men-strength, but didn't stand a chance. We tried everything: WD-40, rust remover, heat and finally a 2.000 NM impact gun, which ultimately ripped the entire bolt off. The thread was fused to the screw and the caliper bracket essentially became one with the wheel hub. _F*ck..._

## Where to get new wheel hubs?

It wasn't a very pleasent moment for all of us, five men standing around the car visibly evaluating our options. There were no after market wheel hubs for my model and I couldn't afford paying for two entirely new parts. Fortunately I discovered an online Shop that sold scrap parts of  crashed cars, which had plenty of listings for wheel hubs. I accepted the risk and bought the parts of a swedish A4 with only 40.000 kilometers on the dash for roughly €500,-. Truly a bargain for such huge parts!

Eventually all parts arrived, wheel hubs, wheel bearings and brake caliper brackets. It seemed like a good day, everything went smoothly, until installing one of the control arms back in. The rubber bearing was brittle and had to be swapped out. We didn't have tools for that and it was easier to just order new control arms as well...

Probably for the third time that month I was at the car part shop in a nearby town, every time with a different order - I don't even want to know what they were thinking about me! xD

## More brake caliper issues?!

But _of course_ the story doesn't end here, as we noticed that the brake fluid was leaking from the OEM brake caliper that Audi changed a few weeks ago. We probably fucked up while fiddling with the screwdriver or something... So I had to order another break caliper. AGAIN. The most painful thing is that Audi's caliper was a complete waste of money, a lot of money, as it's now broken. Days passed and the part finally arrived. In a **terrible** condition. According to Autodoc the caliper should've been a refurbished OEM part, but it still had the original rubber sealant, which I'm scared is going to get brittle in a few months... 

Nevermind, we installed the part, did the electrical work and off I went. The car finally stood on its own four wheel again. _After more than a whole month of pain and suffering._
