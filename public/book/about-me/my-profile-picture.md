# My profile picture

If we remind ourselves of the design chapter from before, you might remember that I learned a lot about UI design. Well, making user interfaces isn't the only thing I started doing. Experimenting with illustrations and logos is also something I enjoy doing ever since.

So I started making my own avatars. And boy, did I make a lot of them. They got simpler and simpler over time, but not in a bad way. The latest version of it is the one you see on the top of this page.

Dragons. Those majestic creatures have always been my favorite. I love their shape, I can let my creativity run wild with them. My latest one is actually based on the logo from LLVM, the compiler framework for the C family and other languages.

# Profile picture evolution

![First Generation](my-profile-picture/version-1.webp?width=150px&align=left&style={"margin-right":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

This was my first every profile picture, probably from when I was around 12 years old and got into the gaming / social media space. The logo is **not** designed by me but was actually "stolen" from the internet, but I didn't know it was a mistake back then. However, I quickly started to design my own logo due to this exact reason:

---

![Second Generation](my-profile-picture/version-2.webp?width=150px&align=right&style={"margin-left":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

I began to experiment with different tools on my computer such as Paint.Net - it had all sorts of effects like grain, gradients, edge detection and more - and I eventually came up with a polygonal styled version of the dragon. There were also different variations of this design, but I definitely like this one the most.

---

![Third Generation](my-profile-picture/version-3-dark.png?theme=dark&width=150px&align=left&style={"margin-right":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})
![Third Generation](my-profile-picture/version-3-light.png?theme=light&width=150px&align=left&style={"margin-right":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

From now on, my profile pictures became simpler and simpler over time, but I still liked the design and it was easy to embed into different places such as my website, etc... I kept this design idea in particular through the next few iterations, probably because it was fairly easy to adapt to get the most out of it.

---

![Fourth Generation](my-profile-picture/version-4.webp?width=150px&align=right&style={"margin-left":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

Version four was more colorful again because it fit the style of my portfolio (which it never made to the public). Essentially it was just a rearranged version of the previous one and **huge** dropshadow (which looks horrendous in my opinion xD). I think this image was the first vectorized one created in Illustrator (although there are better alternatives out there now!)

---

![Fifth Generation](my-profile-picture/version-5.webp?width=150px&align=left&style={"margin-right":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

Moving forward, I adapted the logo again, but this time in a comic/hand-drawn style. The idea actually came to myself during development of another silly mobile game. I remeber it being heavily inspired by _Like a Dino!_ from a design-perspective. I kept this picture for a fairly long time because I really like the texture and I actually felt comfortable using it throughout my socials.

---

![Sixth Generation](my-profile-picture/version-6.webp?width=150px&align=right&style={"margin-left":"1rem","shape-outside":"circle(50%)"})

This is newest version of my dragon logo, the one also used on this portfolio. I became bored from the previous designs and created a flat side-view dragon inspired by the LLVM logo (as explained above). I think it still comes across professional but isn't as "childish" as the last ones. Plus, it integrates really nicely into different locations such as in this website's header! This profile picture is likely going to be the last one I make from the dragon category because I explored a completely different direction lately and have used several other pictures instead.
