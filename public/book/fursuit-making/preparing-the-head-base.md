# Unboxing and assembling the Head Base

![Head Base Package](preparing-the-head-base/head-base-package.webp?width=200px&align=right&border)

My package from Canada finally arrived! I bought the head base from [Yazur's Critter Hub](https://www.etsy.com/at/shop/YazursCrittersHub), a Canadian shop for fursuit materials on Etsy, because I fell in love with the head shape and there were options to buy fitting ears, eyeblanks and an incredible jawset, without having to worry about compatibility issues.

## A "short" stay in the Netherlands

Normally I don't like importing things from countries outside the EU due to unpredictable shipping fees and delivery date, but I didn't really have another option, as the objectively best bases were from America. Within a few days of shipment via ferry, the package was soon in the Netherlands and I thought the remaining delivery would be quick as well, because it's only within the EU from there on. Long story short, delivery to Austria took another 17 days, which was quite a bit longer than expected, making it a total of 28 days. Customs duty was about €60,- which honestly was expected - but I am already kind of scared about the final price of the entire suit, _but hobbies are allowed to dig holes in the purse, right?_

## Glueing the Ears to the Head

![Head Base with Ears](preparing-the-head-base/head-base-with-ears.webp?width=200px&align=right&border)

The very first thing I did was glueing the ears to the head base, mainly to get a better idea of the finished suit and the fur would need to go over the entire thing anyways, so why not. I love the look of the ears - they are bigger than I initially expected, but the sharp style really gives off those fox vibes. They are quite far apart though and are pointing sideways, but that's the recommended position they were designed to be placed at.

For glueing I used any ordinary hot glue, but I am sure there are better alternatives suited for extra strong bonds. Currently, the glue holds up quite well once cooled down, but I definitely must not save on it, as I imagine it won't hold up under lots of pressure and frequent use when only glueing small spots.

## Adding Lining to the Base

![Head Base Package](preparing-the-head-base/inner-lining.webp?width=300px&align=right&border)

To make the head a little bit more comfy on the inside and protect it from sweat, the first thing is to add a thin, breathable layer of fabric made from cotton to the inside of the base. I will also use this layer to glue or sew other items to it, such as an electric fan and rubber head strap (as seen in the pictures).

Cutting out the fabric was by far the most frustrating thing I have done in a long time. Working in such a small and enclosed space turns out to be pretty difficult! Unfortunately, I messed up the measurements of the fabric pretty badly and had to glue a second piece of fabric on top of the first one. I think my mistake was caused by the fact I misaligned the fabric at the nose, which made the entire thing too short to reach the back of the head. In the end I didn't have enough excess fabric to sew the rest of the lining around the neck, so I had to glue it down instead (note the pretty obvious seam through the center of the head base).

## Making the Suit wearable!

![Head Base Package](preparing-the-head-base/head-strap.webp?width=200px&align=right&border)

Now comes the fun part! I get to actually *wear* the suit! Originally, my plan was to glue the strap onto the inner lining, but the head base already included pre-made cutouts for where the head strap should be attached, so I just had to wrap the rubber straps through the loops and sew them tight. The best part is that the strap I ordered from Amazon is adjustable, which helped me immensely to achieve a snug fit for my head shape.

I know, the front strap that goes on the forehead looks far too tight - in fact it spans completely straight - but this fit is necessary so my brow bones don't touch the eyeblanks which would give me one hell of a headache after a few minutes of wearing the suit.

![Head Base Package](preparing-the-head-base/fursuit-head-worn.gif?width=200px&align=right&border)

Actually being able to wear the head for the first time is a pretty good feeling. Of course, it doesn't yet look anything like a finished fursuit, but it definitely helps me imagine what the it will look like once its done. Also the moving jaw is pretty incredible and works like a charm. It is a tiny bit too far away for my chin though, so I am probably going to extend the plating with upholstery foam.

Also the gap between the jaw and the cheeks can be quite large when the mouth is open, so I have to cleverly use long-pile fur to hide it. I'll probably also add lining of matching color to the gap's inside so you can't see the inside.
