# Furring the Head

![All the fur there is](furring-the-head/all-the-fur-there-is.webp?size=half&align=right&border)

Now comes the fun part! I painstakingly cut out each individual pattern with a nail scissors and paid extra attention to not trim the shag. This step sounds very time consuming, but it actually isn't, especially when watching YouTube videos or livestreams in the meantime.

After all the patterns were snipped out, I am now left with a **huge** pile of colored fur on my table waiting to be shaved and sewn together. I opted for hand sewing everything because I need to be a lot more precise than e.g. with the tail and machine sewing would produce a thick ridge along the seam that will be visible through the fur. With a blanket stitch however, all edges are directly connected without excess material, which should result in a completely flat assembly.

However, silly me didn't think that through entirely, so I messed up a bit while cutting out the fur. I left way too much seam allowance initially (~5mm), which would have resulted in bulges and waves in the final result, because the entire thing would have been too large for the head base. Thankfully I caught this issue early on in the process and trimmed away the allowance and cut exactly along my markings. I totally forgot that I didn't need to account for seam allowance when sewing with a blanket stitch! _That's all part of the learning process!_

## Blanket sewing (everything) together

As already mentioned before I am going to hand sew all parts of the head because it gives me way more control and precision over everything. I thought it will take ages to complete, but once I became accustomed to blanket stitching it was a fairly straight-forward process. The most complex part was definitely sewing the face because it was also the most detailled and required high precision:

During stitching however, I noticed that something was wrong. I somehow had way too much fur... So I layed out all individual parts I had and saw that I cut out the same pattern twice. For both sides. I can't even put into words how I just stood there and tried to comprehend what was going on - it felt like my only two braincells were fighting for the third place xD

I got carried away big time, so much that I really didn't notice that I had already cut out this huge part, which is honestly pretty scary, _but whatever..._ I can still make use of these parts by using them as practice material or as backup, but for now they go into the leftovers box.

## Key takeaways

- Don't account for seam allowance when blanket-stitching, as all edges lay directly next to each other without a ridge.
- The best way to hand stitch is to double-up the thread and to knot both ends to keep everything even.
- To start a blanket stitch, pull the needle entirely through both sides of the fur up to a centimeter, then insert the needle between the two threads to secure the start. Then stitch as usual.

_Practice makes perfect!_
