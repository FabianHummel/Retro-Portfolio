# Furring the Head

![All the fur there is](furring-the-head/all-the-fur-there-is.webp?size=half&align=right&border)

Now comes the fun part! I painstakingly cut out each individual pattern with a nail scissors and paid extra attention to not trim the shag. This step sounds very time consuming - _because it is_ - but time flies by quickly when I'm going full-focus or watching YouTube videos in the meantime.

After all the patterns were snipped out, I am now left with a **huge** pile of colored fur on my table waiting to be shaved and sewn together. I opted for hand sewing everything because I need to be a lot more precise than e.g. with the tail and machine sewing would produce a thick ridge along the seam that will be visible through the fur. With a blanket stitch however, all edges are directly connected without excess material, which should result in a completely flat assembly.

However, silly me didn't think that through entirely, so I messed up a bit while cutting out the fur. I left way too much seam allowance initially (~5mm), which would have resulted in bulges and waves in the final result, because the entire thing would have been too large for the head base. Thankfully I caught this issue early on in the process and trimmed away the allowance and cut exactly along my markings. I totally forgot that I didn't need to account for seam allowance when sewing with a blanket stitch! _That's all part of the learning process!_

## Sewing (almost) everything together

As already mentioned before I am going to hand sew all parts of the head because it gives me way more control and precision over everything. I thought it will take ages to complete, but once I became accustomed to blanket stitching it was a fairly straight-forward process. The most complex part was definitely sewing the face because it was also the most detailled and required high precision:

During stitching however, I noticed that something was wrong. I somehow had way too much fur... So I layed out all individual parts I had and- _I cut out the same pattern twice... For both sides._ I can't even put into words how I just stood there and tried to comprehend what was going on - it felt like my only two braincells were fighting for the third place xD

I got carried away big time, so much that I really didn't notice that I had already cut out this huge part, which is honestly pretty scary. _whatever..._ I can still make use of these parts by using them as practice material or as backup, but for now they go into the leftovers box.

### Personal takeaways for hand-sewing

- Don't account for seam allowance when blanket-stitching, as all edges lay directly next to each other without a ridge.
- The best way to hand stitch is to double-up the thread and to knot both ends to keep everything even.
- To start a blanket stitch, pull the needle entirely through both sides of the fur up to a centimeter, then insert the needle between the two threads close to the knot to secure the start. Then stitch as usual.
- _Practice makes perfect!_

## Giving the fur a rough shave

![Rough Shave](furring-the-head/rough-shave.webp?size=half&align=right&border)

I roughly shaved down the long shag with my mother's old clipper she used on trimming our poodle. Starting with 12mm, going shorter step by step until I reached somewhat around 3mm. Making multiple passes is key here, always brushing out the fur after each round. I used a typical hair comb for that, which was sufficient in my case.

I did mess up once though and had to redo a piece. Everything else went super smooth though, which I honestly didn't expect, given I have never had a clipper in my hands.

## Putting everything together

Aligning the fur on the ears properly was always very problematic, so I had to glue the fur on them first - glueing them at last would have been impossible... I even had to cut off one ear to make it fit properly. Every tutorial I watched glued the "sock" from front to back, but mine was way too short in the eyes-area after glueing the ears first. With enough force I could manage to get a snug fit though! This step reminded of making the inner lining - it was unpleasant to say the least...

Unfortunately I don't have footage of the progress here because I went full-focus mode the last two days - I wanted to get the suit ready for today's local meet in St. Pölten!

## Final details

To make the suit complete, I sewed the lining to the fur with a super clean blanket stitch and even left a little overhang so the zipper would be entirely hidden. Around the eyes I added a soft black lining for stylistic purposes and generally refined the shave with a short clipper. Also notice the cute teeth and nose :3

I think the result turned out FIRE!!! 🔥🔥🔥 

No honestly, I am so proud of myself that the suit came out the way it did, especially because I have absolutely no prior experience in anything related to hand crafting something like this! Just look at it!
